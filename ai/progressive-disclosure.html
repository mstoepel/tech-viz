Sandbox 06 · AI systems

Tell the model what exists. Show it only what it needs

An agent with fifty skills can't carry fifty manuals in every prompt. Progressive disclosure is the alternative: an always-loaded core, a one-line index of everything available, and full instructions fetched only when a task calls for them. It costs an extra round trip and moves the whole burden onto the one-liners. Grow the catalogue, switch the loading mode, and watch cost and accuracy.

Engine notes. Same shell as the other sandboxes. The catalogue is twelve real skills plus generated distractors whose bodies cross-reference other domains the way real documentation does ("not for spreadsheets — see xlsx"). The "model" picks a skill by keyword overlap between the task and whatever text is visible to it: descriptions count two per matching word, body text one per occurrence (capped at two), and once the prompt passes about six thousand tokens, matches in the middle of it are discounted — the "lost in the middle" effect measured in real models. Those two mechanisms, incidental matches and fading attention, are why eager loading gets confused as the catalogue grows. Token counts are characters ÷ 4. The curves are illustrative in magnitude and real in shape.
