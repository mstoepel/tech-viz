Retrieval finds what's near. That isn't the same as useful

Retrieval-augmented generation is a search problem with a token budget bolted on. Documents are cut into chunks, each becomes a vector, a question becomes a vector, and the nearest few get pasted into the prompt. Every knob trades one failure for another: chunks too small split the answer, too big bury it, k too small misses, too large drowns it. Ask a question and watch what actually reaches the model.

Engine notes. Same shell as the other sandboxes. The vectors are real TF-IDF over the six documents in the source, compared by cosine; the map is classical multidimensional scaling (power iteration on the double-centred similarity matrix), so two chunks near each other really are similar. Each question's answer is a span of sentences, and a question counts as answered only if every sentence of that span reaches the model contiguously — which is why a chunk boundary through the middle of an answer breaks it. dense expands the query through a hand-written synonym table, standing in for what a trained embedding model knows and a bag of words cannot; rerank is a second scorer that reads the whole chunk instead of its vector. Position weighting uses the same "lost in the middle" discount as the context-engineering sandbox. Tokens are characters ÷ 4.
