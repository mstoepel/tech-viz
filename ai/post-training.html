Pretraining learns the world. Post-training learns what you want

A pretrained model is a superb predictor of what text comes next and a poor assistant. Everything after that — supervised fine-tuning, a reward model, RLHF, DPO — is a series of nudges to the same weights so that the likely answer becomes the wanted one. This page is one prompt, seven possible answers, and the real update rules, one step at a time.

Engine notes. Same shell as the other sandboxes, with clickable controls. The policy is a softmax over seven fixed answers, which stands in for a distribution over all possible text. Every update is the real gradient for that toy: SFT is the cross-entropy gradient toward the demonstration; the reward model is a Bradley–Terry fit to simulated labeler comparisons, where the labelers decide by true quality plus a mild taste for enthusiasm plus noise; RLHF is the mirror-descent form of the policy gradient of expected reward minus β·KL to the reference (same fixed point as the vanilla gradient, but it can reach low-probability answers in a handful of steps); DPO is the DPO gradient on the same comparisons. The "true" reward is the quantity the labelers were noisy proxies for. Nothing here is a language model; everything here is the arithmetic language models are post-trained with.
