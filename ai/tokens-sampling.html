The model reads in pieces and writes by throwing dice

Two things happen at the edges of every language model that nobody sees. On the way in, text is chopped into pieces by a tokenizer that was built from letter frequencies, not meaning. On the way out, the model produces a probability for every piece in its vocabulary, and a sampler reshapes that distribution and rolls. Both halves here are real: a byte-pair tokenizer trained in front of you, and a small language model behind the bars.

Engine notes. Same shell as the other sandboxes. The tokenizer is real byte-pair encoding trained on the 230-word corpus in the source: start from characters (with a ▁ marking a word start), merge the most frequent adjacent pair, repeat; the slider replays the merges in the order they were learned. The language model is a trigram model with backoff over those tokens — a genuine probability distribution over the vocabulary, just a very small and very local one. Temperature, top-k and top-p are applied exactly as a real sampler applies them: divide log-probabilities by T, softmax, truncate, renormalise, draw. Token ids are vocabulary indices; real vocabularies are ~100k pieces, not ~200.
